# Safety

What can go wrong when an AI agent uses these tools, what the software does about it, what it cannot do, where your secrets live, what to do if a machine is lost, and where XO's terms and regions bite. Plain words first; sources in brackets. **Nothing here is legal or financial advice.**

Bracketed tags cite [docs/phase1-gap-closing.md](phase1-gap-closing.md): `[risk N]` is its section 7, `[A1]` to `[A12]` its section 9 amendments, `[O5]` its section 9.4, `[D3]` its section 8, and ids such as `[AU5]` or `[OR2]` its section 3 assumptions. `[gate 7]` is [phase0-gate-results.md](phase0-gate-results.md).

Read [README.md](../README.md) first for what is built. This page matters only for the parts that touch money: funding, trading, redeeming, withdrawing and creating markets. The read tools and the draft linter move nothing.

## The one-paragraph version

Your PayBox wallet signs; this software prepares, checks and sends. The checks (a $10 per order and $50 per day limit, a short order expiry, flags that start off, a kill switch) are **enforced by this software only**, so a hijacked or prompt-injected agent that is allowed to sign through PayBox can ignore them. The only mode in which a person sees each signature is approval-gated signing, and even there the person usually sees an opaque digest. So: use a dedicated PayBox credential for XO, keep only money you can afford to lose behind it, use approval-gated mode, and treat every XO write as unproven until the live tests have been run (nobody has run them against the real XO service yet). [`src/relay/contract.ts` `SAFETY_NOTICES`; `docs/phase1-gap-closing.md` sections 5.2, 7 and 9 A4]

## Threat model

| Who or what | What they could do | What the software does | What remains |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A prompt-injected or compromised agent** (the most likely problem: the agent reads market text written by strangers) | Ask PayBox to sign something this software never prepared: an order, a user operation, a login message, even an ordinary transaction. It can then talk to XO or to a chain directly | Market text is returned only in fields marked `trust=untrusted-user-content` and, in summaries a human reads, inside a fixed slot `[market title (untrusted): "..."]` of at most 80 characters with control characters stripped. The relay verifies that a signature submitted to it recovers to your wallet and matches what it prepared. Account and maker addresses are derived from your wallet address, never taken from the agent [`docs/tool-contract.md`; `src/tools/relay-tool-kit.ts`; `docs/phase1-gap-closing.md` 5.4 invariants] | **The caps do not stop this.** `raw` signing has no domain separation: an agent can ask for a signature over a digest of its choosing, and an approver sees an opaque digest [A4] |
| **A deceived human approver** | Approves a digest that does something other than what the summary said | Each prepare returns a decoded summary and a `digest_tag` (first 16 hex digits) to compare with PayBox's window [A4] | The tag guards against accidents only. A hostile agent can show one thing and ask for another; approval fatigue is real [risk 4] |
| **A stranger who steals a signed order or user operation** | A signed order is a bearer instrument until it expires (there is no on-chain cancel); a signed user operation has no expiry | Every order is GTD with a short expiry (300 s by default, ceiling one hour, never none); submit refuses an order with less than 90 s of life left; unsent signed operations are meant to be sent at once [OR2; UO10; `src/xo/signing/expiry.ts`] | Whether XO requires its own login header on `POST /order`, which decides whether a leaked order can be posted by a stranger, is unknown [risk 6] |
| **A stranger who steals your XO API credentials** | Read your private order data and send cancels | Stored encrypted (below). They cannot place orders: every order needs your wallet's signature (medium confidence) [AU5] | No tool rotates or deletes the credentials; losing them may be unrecoverable [AU3; `docs/phase1-gap-closing.md` 5.1 "Not built"] |
| **A bug or hostile change at XO** | XO can change anything: the paymaster that sponsors gas, who may create markets, the exchange contract (upgradeable), the fee ceiling; void markets; end your access "with or without cause" [XS1; risk 3] | Tripwires watch pinned on-chain properties; on a change the relay goes read-only and alerts. Unknown values fail closed (the fee bound falls back to 8%) | Tripwires turn most surprises into "stop", not into "safe". An upgrade to the exchange could pull whatever you have approved |
| **XO's off-chain stack not accepting you** | Funds that cannot be used or taken out | Test amounts only until each leg is proven; funding refuses an undeployed account; no resending [FU6; FU9; D3] | **There is no verified way to take money out of XO.** `xo_withdraw` is off and rests on an untested hypothesis. Funding is one-way until test T4 passes |
| **PayBox behaving differently from the docs** | Autonomous grants may or may not sign without a human; `raw` may or may not sign a bare digest; PayBox's own docs contradict each other here | The design works under both outcomes and recommends approval-gated mode; test T0 records the truth [PB1 to PB4; `docs/research/06-paybox-platform.md` section 6] | Until T0 runs, assume the worst |
| **Malware or another user on your machine** | Read process memory or the environment, including a master passphrase | File modes of 0600, no secrets in logs, a store that refuses to start without its passphrase | Nothing here defends a compromised machine |
| **Mistakes in this code** | Anything | Every write path starts off, sits behind a kill switch, fails closed on unknown, and has a failing-case test; a log scanner test checks that secrets do not reach output [XS5] | It is new software that has never touched the real service. Fund small |

## What the caps protect, and what they do not

**What they are.** $10 per order and $50 per day by default (USD, counted on the worst case: for a buy that includes a fee allowance of 10% of the principal, the cap multiplier of 1,000 bps; the fee tripwire still compares the fee XO really charged with the 800 bps bound). You can lower them or raise them up to compiled-in ceilings of $100 and $500 (`XO_MAX_ORDER_USD`, `XO_MAX_DAILY_USD`; `src/config.ts`). Funding has its own limits: a 3 USD test amount until a first deposit has been seen to arrive and be credited, and a $10 total (then $50 per day) until the exit test passes. A market-creation seed has its own cap of $10.80 (a $10.00 stake times the 1.08 fee multiplier), and the $1 creation fee counts toward the day. There is no limit on how many markets you create; the daily cap, per-attempt approval and the creation flag (off by default) are the brakes [`docs/phase1-gap-closing.md` A3, D5; `relay/config.ts`]. The daily ledger is durable: a restart does not reset it, and a reservation is released only after the item's expiry has passed and the order is confirmed gone [A2].

**What they do protect.** An agent that behaves normally and goes through these tools: a typo, a hallucinated size, a loop, a market whose text tries to argue the agent into a huge order. The tool refuses before anything is signed.

**What they do not protect.** An agent that can reach `request_wallet_sign` on your credential can sign a login message, mint its own XO credentials, sign orders and operations itself and talk to XO directly, bypassing this software [`docs/phase1-gap-closing.md` 5.2]. Under an `autonomous` grant nothing is shown to anyone before PayBox signs. Approval-gated signing narrows this to "a person sees each request", which helps against unattended misuse but not against a person approving an opaque digest, and the PayBox owner key is also that wallet's Base address, so one blind approval can move its Base funds as well, not only the XO account [A4]. That is why:

1. The XO owner should be a **dedicated PayBox credential** that holds only what you will fund (open item O1; the build assumes it).
2. Use **approval-gated mode** for any credential with real money. `autonomous` is acceptable only for a throwaway credential with test amounts.
3. Prefer typed-data signing over `raw` wherever XO accepts both (A4).
4. Fund in small steps, and not above the test amount until T3a has recorded both delivery and credit.

Every write tool's description says the caps are advisory against a compromised agent; if you ever see one that does not, that is a bug to report.

## Where secrets live

| Secret | Where | Protection | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your wallet's private key | PayBox, never this software | Not here | README, `docs/phase1-gap-closing.md` 5.2 |
| XO API credentials (key, secret, passphrase) | `secrets/` inside the data directory you name | AES-256-GCM; key derived from your master passphrase with scrypt (2^17 cost, 128 MiB); your account address is bound in as associated data, so a record copied elsewhere will not open; file mode 0600; atomic writes; an encrypted backup in `backups/` is written and read back **before** a new credential may be used | `relay/crypto.ts`, `relay/store.ts` |
| Master passphrase (hosted relay, `pnpm relay`) | Your environment, or a terminal prompt; **never a file read by this code**, and never on disk by this software | Held in memory only; minimum 16 characters; must differ from the relay key | `relay/config.ts` |
| Master key (local mode, `dist/local.mjs`, the day-zero mode) | **By default a file**: 32 random bytes in `master.key` inside the data directory (mode 0600), unless you set `XO_MASTER_KEY` (for example from your OS keychain) | The file protects the vault against someone who gets only a copy of the files (a backup, a synced folder). It does **not** protect against malware or any process running as you, including an agent with a shell or file tool: it can read `master.key` and the vault and decrypt your XO credential. Set `XO_MASTER_KEY` from a keychain for more. What the XO credential can do is limited (it cannot place an order without your wallet's signature), but treat it as readable by anything running as you | `src/local/master-key.ts` |
| Relay key (`XO_RELAY_KEY`), hosted-sandbox mode only | Environment variable | Held in a wrapper that prints as `[redacted]`; never echoed in errors; rotatable with a second accepted key (`XO_RELAY_KEY_NEXT`) | `src/config.ts`, `relay/config.ts` |
| Optional Base RPC URL | Environment variable | Treated as a secret (providers embed keys in URLs) | `relay/config.ts` |
| Ledger, prepared items, recorded facts, tripwire state | Data directory, as a checksummed journal plus snapshot | Not secret; no signature and no plaintext secret is ever written there | `relay/store.ts` |
| Relay audit log | `audit.log` in the data directory (append-only JSON lines, mode 0600, rotated by size) | Redacted; records tags, not signatures | `relay/audit.ts`; XS5 |
| Plugin audit line | stderr, one line per call | Free text is never logged | `docs/tool-contract.md` "Audit" |
| Signed orders and operations before they are sent | The agent's context and PayBox | Short expiry; single-use prepared items | above |

The hosted relay's data directory must be named explicitly (no default). Local mode has a default (your OS's per-user data directory, created mode 0700), and `XO_DATA_DIR` overrides it; either way, keep it outside any repository. Keep it outside the repository, back it up as you would a wallet file (the backups are encrypted but useless without the passphrase), and keep the passphrase somewhere other than next to it. Also: this repository's own hygiene test fails the build if a personal identifier, a home path or a session path appears in any committed text (`test/hygiene-identifiers.test.ts`); no request the software makes carries one.

## If a machine is lost or compromised

Work from the cheapest, most reversible step down. Do 1 and 2 first.

1. **Cut the agent off.** In PayBox, revoke the connection or grant that lets agents use the dedicated credential, and end the sessions on the lost device (PayBox's own pages: <https://docs.paybox.sh>; this project does not call PayBox and I have not checked the exact steps).
2. **Stop trading on chain, from a machine you trust.** Run `xo_revoke_trading` (prepare, sign, submit). It sets the exchange's USDC.e allowance to 0 and removes its position-token approval, needs only a deployed account, and is sent without a paymaster. Signed orders that were not yet posted can then no longer settle. This needs `ENABLE_USEROP_SUBMIT` on and the kill switch off on that machine, because the kill switch blocks the revoke too [`src/tools/userop.ts`; `relay/userops.ts`; `src/relay/flags.ts`].
3. **Move what is left in the owner wallet.** The owner key is also that wallet's Base address, so any funds still on Base behind the credential are exposed to a blind approval. Move them with PayBox to a credential on a clean device.
4. **Change the passphrase and the keys.** The master passphrase protects the stolen data directory; if the thief had both, treat the XO credentials as read-compromised. Change `XO_RELAY_KEY` (hosted mode; use `XO_RELAY_KEY_NEXT` to rotate without downtime), and the Base RPC provider key if you used one.
5. **XO's credentials.** There is no tool to rotate or delete them (not built), and whether XO lets you revoke a key is unknown (the delete route's auth level is unverified [AU3]). What a thief with only these credentials can do is read your private order data and send cancels; they cannot place orders without your wallet's signature (medium confidence [AU5]).
6. **Outstanding orders** expire on their own (default 300 s, ceiling 1 h). There is no on-chain cancel.
7. **Be honest about what you cannot undo.** The XO account is bound to the owner credential; there is no owner rotation (AD8). If the *credential* is compromised rather than a machine, the XO account cannot be handed to a new owner, and there is no verified way to withdraw. That is why funding stays small.

## XO terms and region exposure (open item O5)

This is a summary of what the research found, not an opinion on whether you may use XO. XO's terms are undated and can change with or without notice; the privacy policy found was stale. Read them yourself and check your own situation.

- **Regions.** XO lists restricted regions: the US, UK, France, Singapore, Ontario and sanctioned territories, enforced by an IP check at login and at transaction time. VPN, proxy or Tor masking is prohibited. In local mode your own connection is the egress address; I found no region check in this code, so nothing here checks for you [`docs/phase0-gate-results.md` gate 7].
- **Bots and automation.** Nothing bans bots, but the clause says automated access "must comply with these Terms and not overwhelm or misuse the system". The software throttles itself to 4 requests per second per host. No XO statement exists on agent-operated accounts, and the project does not ask [gate 7; XS4].
- **Wash trading, manipulation, influence.** Trading with yourself or colluding to create artificial activity is prohibited, as is trading where you have direct or indirect influence over a market's outcome. Every sampled market resolves by a creator-specified resolver, so a creator trading its own market is exposed to the influence clause. The orderbook has self-trade prevention (self-matches cancel and earn no rebate). The plan's author decision that self-trading is "always allowed" is a statement about this software, not about XO's rules [gate 7; `docs/plan.md`].
- **One account per user.** The derived account is a single account per owner by construction (AD9).
- **Creating markets** (off by default, built and tested only against a mock): XO's terms describe a badge, at most one market per calendar week and admin review, and a $1.00 non-refundable fee. The weekly limit is not enforced on chain and the research saw it exceeded often, but it remains invocable, and XO can void markets, revoke creation or close the path. There is no creation cadence cap in this software (a user decision), so the terms are the only limit. Void settlement is 50/50 (terms) or pro-rata (a lifecycle guide), and 13.7% of observed attempts never activated [gate 7; `docs/phase1-gap-closing.md` risk 11, CR10].
- **Position.** `docs/phase1-gap-closing.md` section 9.4 records O5 as: *"Accept XO terms, badge-rule and jurisdiction exposure for creation, and where the relay's egress address sits"*, default **not accepted; creation stays off by default**. An earlier sentence that "the user accepted the exposure" was an inference by a synthesis step, not a recorded decision. If you turn creation on, that is your decision about your exposure.
- **Contact.** This project does not contact XO about any of this, and no tool here calls any XO feedback channel. So there is no safe harbour from XO; the live tests are real actions on a real service [risk 12, risk 18].

## Honest summary of what is unproven

The mechanics were checked offline against XO's own contract source and thousands of on-chain records, and the code is tested against a local mock and a local chain with the real contract bytecode. What nobody has seen: XO's bundler admitting a first operation from your account, XO's registry and login accepting it, XO crediting a deposit, any real order from this software, PayBox signing a bare digest, any exit. Live tests T0 to T8 settle these ([quickstart.md](quickstart.md) section 8). Until they are run, each write tool's result lists the facts it still depends on; read that list.

## Reporting a problem

- **What this repository publishes:** no private security contact. If you found a way to make the software sign, send or spend something it should not (a cap bypass, a secret in an output, a market text that moved a tool), do not post working details, key material or your data directory in public. Use whatever private channel the person who gave you this code provides; if there is none, ask for one before sending details.
- **What to include:** the tool name, the error code (`flag_off`, `cap_exceeded`, `ambiguous`, ...), the `prepared_id` if shown, the request id (`rid`) and the relevant lines of `audit.log` (already redacted), your Node and pnpm versions, and the commit. Never include the master passphrase, the XO credentials, a signature or the contents of `secrets/` or `backups/`.
- **If money may have moved:** run `xo_account` for the derived account, `xo_order_status` and `xo_userop_status` for anything ambiguous, and do not resubmit; an ambiguous send may already have landed.
- **Problems with XO or PayBox themselves** belong with them, through their own channels. This software never contacts either on your behalf.
